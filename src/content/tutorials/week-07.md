## Activity

Observe a queue anywhere strangers are waiting their turn — a counter, a bus
stop, a food stall — and note where the agreement about whose turn it is
looks strongest (a clear single-file front) versus weakest (an ambiguous
cluster, a fork that never re-merged cleanly).

## Record your reaction

Note any moment where the queue forked or merged, and how the people
involved resolved — or failed to resolve — it.

## Reflection

Using the lecture's cutting spectrum (asking first, exploiting an ambiguous
merge, walking straight to the front), place the example you observed on
that spectrum and justify where it sits. [Assignment 2](/assessments/assignment-2/)
is due this week and asks for exactly this kind of evidence.
