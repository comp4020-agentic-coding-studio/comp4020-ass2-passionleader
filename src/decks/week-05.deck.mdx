## Seat-saving as a property system

- claims without enforcement: what makes an unattended jacket work as a sign
- scarcity as the variable that changes everything

```notes
Ask: has anyone in the room ever contested a saved seat? Almost nobody has —
that's the point.
```

---

## What breaks the claim

A single occupied seat in an empty café reads as inviolable. The same
jacket, same café, at peak hour — reads completely differently.

---

{/* _class: centered */}

## Wrong vs. right

**Wrong:** describing it as "he saved a seat" — a single verdict that skips
past the two variables that actually decide whether a claim like that
holds: how long the seat sits empty, and how scarce seats are at that
moment.

**Right:** isolating those two variables separately — time-empty and
scarcity — before deciding whether the claim was reasonable.

## Quiz

**Q1.** What single variable does this week identify as changing whether a
seat-saving claim holds?

*Scarcity — how much competition there is for the seat at that moment.*

---

**Q2.** Why does the course call seat-saving "a property system that runs
entirely on convention"?

*Because it has no deed, no lock, and no enforcement mechanism beyond other
people choosing to honour it.*

---

**Q3.** Why does this week say "he saved a seat" is an incomplete
description on its own, whatever the verdict turns out to be?

*Because it skips past the two variables that actually decide the case —
how long the seat sits empty, and how scarce seats are at that moment —
and jumps straight to a verdict without isolating either one first.*
