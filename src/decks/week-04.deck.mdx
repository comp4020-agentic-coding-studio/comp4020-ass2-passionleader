## Bad manner vs. good manner

**Bad manner:** A student trying to read at a library table, a phone
conversation dragging past a minute two seats over, lets out a loud "shhh"
audible across the whole floor — technically aimed at the noise, but now
everyone nearby is looking at the shusher, not the person on the phone.

**Good manner:** That same student walking the three steps over and asking,
quietly, "would you mind stepping into the hallway?" — smaller than the
shush, and it actually solves the problem instead of just announcing it.

## Quiz

**Q1.** Why is a library's ambient noise floor described as "a design goal,
not an accident"?

*Because the room is built to be quiet by default, which is exactly what
makes any deviation from it easy to measure.*

---

**Q2.** What makes the shush a "disproportionate" response?

*It is, itself, louder and more socially disruptive than most of what it's
responding to — yet a room still tolerates it more than it tolerates the
original noise.*

---

**Q3.** A five-minute phone call goes unchallenged; a ten-second shush in
response draws visible reactions. What does this asymmetry tell you about
how a room enforces its own norms?

*Enforcement itself carries a bigger, more visible social cost than the
violation it's answering — which is part of why norms like this one are so
rarely enforced directly.*
