# Managing Sound in the Public Restroom

## SLOP2950 · Week 10

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> Everyone present agrees, by unspoken consensus, to act as though the room

---

## A fragile, high-effort norm

- taps run to mask sound both parties know isn't actually masked
- conversations that would be casual anywhere else drop to nothing
- nobody acknowledges any of it — the system depends on that

---

## The tap-as-mask

A countermeasure everyone knows doesn't work. Compare the shush in week 4 —

## Bad manner vs. good manner

**Bad manner:** Taking a phone call in a restroom stall at full volume, no
tap running, no attempt to lower a voice — loud enough that everyone at the
sinks catches both the topic and the name being discussed.

**Good manner:** That same call, but with the tap turned on before it
continues and the voice dropped to near a whisper — a countermeasure that
fools nobody, but is exactly the effort the room's fragile pretence depends
on everyone making.

## Quiz

**Q1.** What is "the restroom's central pretence"?

*That everyone present agrees, without ever saying so, to act as though the
room is silent — even though it obviously isn't.*

---

**Q2.** How does the tap-as-mask compare to the shush from week 4?

*Both are countermeasures everyone recognises don't actually work, and both
still get used anyway — the shush because it's louder than what it answers,
the tap because the silence it's protecting isn't real either.*

---

**Q3.** What does a single acknowledged breach in this space typically cost,
compared to maintaining the pretence?

*It costs a moment of shared discomfort — smaller, usually, than the
ongoing effort of maintaining a fiction everyone already knows isn't true.*
