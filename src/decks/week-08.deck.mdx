## Crowd flow as fluid dynamics

- a rough average speed, a rough average following distance
- gradual changes: absorbed easily
- sudden changes: they aren't

## Measuring the pile-up

How far back the disruption propagates, as a function of footpath width and
crowd density — and how many people caught in it ever learn why.

---

{/* _class: centered */}

## Wrong vs. right

**Wrong:** describing it as "he stopped without warning" — collapsing the
stop itself and the pile-up it caused into a single narrative, so the two
are never separated.

**Right:** isolating the stop as one discrete event, then separately
describing the propagation it caused — two observations, not one.

## Quiz

**Q1.** Why is the sudden stop described as "the cleanest one to measure"
among this course's case studies?

*It's one of the few genuinely instantaneous disruptions the course studies
— it has a clear single moment of cause, unlike most other norms here.*

---

**Q2.** What two variables does the course say the pile-up propagates as a
function of?

*Footpath width and crowd density.*

---

**Q3.** Why does the course treat "he stopped without warning" as a poorly
isolated case description?

*It fuses the stop itself with the pile-up that followed into a single
narrative, instead of describing the two as separate, sequential events.*
