## Measuring the pile-up

How far back the disruption propagates, as a function of footpath width and
crowd density — and how many people caught in it ever learn why.

## Bad manner vs. good manner

**Bad manner:** Walking through a crowded footpath at peak hour, someone
stops dead in the middle of the flow to check a phone notification, without
stepping aside first — the three people right behind them have to
stutter-step or collide, and the five behind those three do the same a
second later.

**Good manner:** That same person, feeling the pull to check their phone,
stepping to the side against a shopfront first — the flow behind them never
even breaks stride.

## Quiz

**Q1.** Why is the sudden stop described as "the cleanest one to measure"
among this course's case studies?

*It's one of the few genuinely instantaneous disruptions the course studies
— it has a clear single moment of cause, unlike most other norms here.*

---

**Q2.** What two variables does the course say the pile-up propagates as a
function of?

*Footpath width and crowd density.*

---

**Q3.** Two people both stop to check a phone notification on a crowded
footpath — one mid-flow, one stepped aside first. Why does only one of them
cause a pile-up?

*Stepping aside removes the stop from the main flow entirely, so nobody
behind has to react to it — stopping mid-flow forces everyone directly
behind to stutter-step or collide, and that reaction propagates backward
through the crowd.*
