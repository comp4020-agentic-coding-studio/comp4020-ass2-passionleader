## Three signals a room actually reads

1. **Timing** — does it interrupt someone speaking
2. **Target** — was it directed anywhere in particular
3. **Aftermath** — was it acknowledged afterward

```notes
These three map directly onto the method: timing and target help you
isolate the act, aftermath is where you describe what happens next.
```

---

## The deniable cough

A cough that a room privately reads as deliberate, while remaining
technically indistinguishable from an accident.

This week catalogues that ambiguity — it does not teach you to produce it.

---

{/* _class: centered */}

## Wrong vs. right

**Wrong:** writing the case up as "a cough directed at someone" — timing,
target, and aftermath fused into a single verdict before any of the three
signals has actually been isolated.

**Right:** isolating the three signals separately first — did it interrupt,
was it aimed, was it acknowledged — and only combining them into a reading
afterward.

## Quiz

**Q1.** Which of the three signals (timing, target, aftermath) is most
directly about isolating the act itself, rather than describing what
happened next?

*Timing and target — aftermath belongs to "describe."*

---

**Q2.** True or false: this week's method is meant to teach students how to
produce an undetectable deliberate cough.

*False — it analyses why the line between accident and message is hard to
prove, not how to blur it on purpose.*

---

**Q3.** A colleague coughs once, directly after you finish a sentence, and
says nothing afterward. Which signal is most ambiguous here?

*Aftermath — the lack of acknowledgment is exactly what a room can't
resolve on its own.*
