## An unpriced commons

There's more space in a carriage than any timetable accounts for, and no
mechanism except posture decides who gets how much of it.

---

{/* _class: centered */}

## Wrong vs. right

**Wrong:** writing the case up as "he took up too much space" — a summary
verdict that never isolates which single input actually did the work: grip
width, seat spread, or volume.

**Right:** isolating one contributing input at a time — grip width, say —
and describing what changed when that one variable, and only that one, was
different.

## Quiz

**Q1.** What does this week mean by describing transit space as an "unpriced
commons"?

*There's no market or rule allocating carriage space — only posture, and
whatever a passenger is willing to claim.*

---

**Q2.** How does week 6's version of claimed space differ from week 3's
elevator?

*The elevator fixes everyone's position for the whole ride; on transport,
claimed space keeps recalculating stop by stop as people board and leave.*

---

**Q3.** Name two things listed this week that cost another passenger
something without breaking any actual rule.

*Any two of: the bag on the empty seat, a wide pole grip, or headphone
volume pitched to be heard rather than merely audible.*
