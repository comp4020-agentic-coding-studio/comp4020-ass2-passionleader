## The audience-less nuisance

Most of this course's etiquette is enforced by an audience in real time.
Littering mostly isn't — which is what makes it a useful, different kind of
case.

---

## Threshold litter

What accumulates in the last few metres before a bin — and what that
distance actually tells you about tolerance for inconvenience.

## Bad manner vs. good manner

**Bad manner:** Walking past a public bin on the footpath, close enough to
reach it without breaking stride, and letting an empty drink cup fall to
the pavement instead — leaving it for whoever cleans the street, or nobody.

**Good manner:** Carrying that same cup the extra three steps to the bin
and setting it inside — a smaller effort than the one just avoided.

## Quiz

**Q1.** Why does the course call littering an "audience-less nuisance"?

*Because the person who benefits from the act is rarely watched doing it,
unlike most of the course's other case studies.*

---

**Q2.** What does "threshold litter" refer to?

*Litter that accumulates in the last few metres before a bin — a rough
measure of exactly how much extra effort people won't make.*

---

**Q3.** In this week's framing, who usually pays the cost of a dropped
wrapper?

*Someone other than the person who dropped it — a council worker, a
passer-by, or whoever's shoe finds it first — rarely the original person,
and rarely right away.*
